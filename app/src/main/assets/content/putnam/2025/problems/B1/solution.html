Fix a red point RRR. Every circle centered at RRR contains at most two green points: otherwise three green points on that circle would be noncollinear, and their circumcenter RRR would have to be green.

Assume for contradiction that there are also a green point GGG, and write d=RG>0d=RG>0d=RG>0. Choose three distinct radii for circles centered at RRR and three distinct radii for circles centered at GGG, all in (d/2,d)(d/2,d)(d/2,d). Choose them generically so that no intersection point is repeated. Every R-circle meets every G-circle in two points, because each pair of radii has sum greater than ddd and difference smaller than ddd. Thus there are 2⋅3⋅3=182\cdot3\cdot3=182⋅3⋅3=18 distinct intersection points.

Each of the three circles centered at RRR contains at most two green intersection points, so at most six of the 18 points are green. Applying the same observation with the colors reversed, each G-circle contains at most two red points, so at most six of the 18 points are red. But every intersection is one of the two colors, giving 18≤6+618\le6+618≤6+6, a contradiction. Hence no red and green points coexist, and all points have the same color.
