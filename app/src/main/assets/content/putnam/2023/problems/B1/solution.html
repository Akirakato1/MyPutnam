The number of reachable configurations is (m+n−2m−1)\binom{m+n-2}{m-1}(m−1m+n−2​).

Initially the unoccupied squares are the last column and the last row. Listed from (1,n)(1,n)(1,n) to (m,1)(m,1)(m,1), they form a monotone path of squares with m−1m-1m−1 down steps and n−1n-1n−1 left steps.

A legal move occurs in a 2-by-22\text{-by-}22-by-2 block whose northwest square is occupied and whose other three squares are empty. Sliding the coin to the southeast square replaces one local corner of the empty-square path by the other corner. Thus after every legal move the empty squares still form exactly one such monotone path.

Because the occupied squares are the complement of the path, the path determines the entire coin configuration. Hence distinct reachable configurations inject into the set of these paths.

Conversely, every monotone path is reachable. Work backward from a target path. Unless it is the initial path, it contains a left-then-down corner lying strictly away from the initial boundary. The southeast square at that corner is occupied, while the other three squares of the corresponding 2-by-22\text{-by-}22-by-2 block are empty. Moving that coin northwest reverses one legal move and decreases the area between the target path and the initial path by one.

Repeating reaches the initial path, so reversing the sequence reaches the target configuration. Therefore the path correspondence is bijective. Finally, choosing the positions of the m−1m-1m−1 down steps among all m+n−2m+n-2m+n−2 steps gives the stated binomial coefficient.
