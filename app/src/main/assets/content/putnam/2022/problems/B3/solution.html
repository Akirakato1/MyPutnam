Yes. In fact, every positive real number is red after at most two recolorings.

A one-step lemma

Suppose that ddd is blue after a recoloring. In the preceding coloring, every pair of points at distance ddd had opposite colors. Hence the four points

d,2d,3d,4dd,\quad 2d,\quad 3d,\quad 4dd,2d,3d,4d

alternated in color. In particular, ddd and 4d4d4d had opposite colors, so 5d/25d/25d/2 shared a color with one of them. Its distance from each is 3d/23d/23d/2, so 3d/23d/23d/2 became red.

Likewise, 2d2d2d and 3d3d3d had opposite colors, so 5d/25d/25d/2 shared a color with one of them. Its distance from each is d/2d/2d/2, so d/2d/2d/2 also became red. We have proved:

d blue ⟹d/2 and 3d/2 red.d\text{ blue }\Longrightarrow d/2\text{ and }3d/2\text{ red}.d blue ⟹d/2 and 3d/2 red.

The second recoloring

The lemma first shows that no distance can be blue in two consecutive colorings. Indeed, if ddd is blue, then d/2d/2d/2 and 3d/23d/23d/2 are red; these two points are distance ddd apart, so ddd is red after the next recoloring.

Assume for contradiction that some ddd is blue after two recolorings. It must have been red after the first. Since it becomes blue at the second step, the colors of d,2d,3d,4dd,2d,3d,4dd,2d,3d,4d after the first step alternate. Therefore 2d2d2d and 4d4d4d are blue after the first recoloring.

Apply the lemma in that coloring to the blue number 4d4d4d. It says that 2d2d2d is red, contradicting the conclusion above. Thus no number is blue after the second recoloring, as required.
